About My Journey

I am building my career around Data Science, Statistics, Artificial Intelligence, and computational research. My goal is not simply to acquire a collection of technical skills, but to develop the mathematical understanding, scientific discipline, and practical ability required to solve difficult problems.

I am particularly interested in the intersection of data science with finance, remote sensing, digital image processing, speech processing, and other computational fields where mathematics and real-world data meet.

Research is an important part of my long-term direction. I want to continue into higher studies not merely for another qualification, but for the opportunity to study difficult questions deeply, work with researchers, develop stronger mathematical foundations, and contribute something genuinely useful to knowledge.

Long-Term Vision

My long-term objective is to build a career that combines technical depth, research, leadership, and continuous learning. I intend to pursue higher education as my understanding develops and gradually move toward research-intensive work in data science, artificial intelligence, statistics, and quantitative applications.

I want to remain a student of the field even after formal education ends. Technology changes quickly, but the ability to learn, reason, investigate, and adapt remains valuable across generations of technology.

How I Approach Difficult Work

I aim to cultivate the mentality of someone who can operate effectively when the problem is difficult, information is incomplete, and the outcome is uncertain.

That means preparing seriously, maintaining discipline when motivation disappears, questioning assumptions, paying attention to details, and taking ownership of the final result.

I admire the combination of corporate leadership and special-forces-style discipline: strategic thinking, preparation, calm execution, teamwork, accountability, adaptability, and the ability to keep functioning under pressure.

In research, this translates into a different kind of discipline: formulate the problem carefully, understand the literature, test assumptions, reproduce results, accept negative results, and follow evidence wherever it leads.

I am trying to build a professional identity around high standards, intellectual honesty, resilience, preparation, and continuous improvement.

Books That Have Influenced My Thinking

The Art of Doing Science and Engineering

Richard Hamming

A book about scientific thinking, problem selection, creativity, persistence, and the habits required to produce meaningful technical work.

Introduction to Linear Algebra

Gilbert Strang

A major influence on how I think about linear algebra, mathematical structure, and the connection between mathematics and applications.

Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!

Richard P. Feynman

A reminder that curiosity, independent thinking, experimentation, and the willingness to question assumptions are powerful parts of scientific work.

The Feynman Lectures on Physics

Richard P. Feynman, Robert B. Leighton & Matthew Sands

An enduring source of inspiration for approaching difficult concepts from first principles and developing physical intuition.
